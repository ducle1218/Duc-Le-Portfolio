The Electrifying Tempest — Engineering Team

Wind Turbine Competition

A team project where we tuned blade length, shape, twist, and pitch on a wind turbine to squeeze out as much electrical power as we could.

Peak: 7.4 Watts Team: Marco, Duc, Connor

MATERIAL & SHAPE STUDIES

Tested a bunch of materials and blade shapes. Cardboard beat foam, chipboard, and cardstock by a clear margin. Rectangular and curved blades outperformed pointy ones, and the best weight balance landed about 1.5 inches from the bottom of the blade.

OPTIMAL CONFIGURATIONS

  • ✓ Blade Twist: 10° to 30° (prevents drag)
  • ✓ Blade Pitch: 0° to 5°
  • ✓ Blade Count: 3 blades (6 caused too much drag)

FINAL OUTCOME

The final prototype (March 16, 2023) consisted of 3 cardboard blades, 14 inches long and 3 inches wide, curved at 10°, with 0° pitch.

Result: Generated an average of 3 Volts and 7.4 Watts, maximizing efficiency through reduced aerodynamic drag and optimized surface area.

Iterative Design & Testing Log

Chronological progression of the wind turbine prototyping process

March 6, 2023

Initial Design & Test (0 Watts)

Tested 3 chipboard blades (3" x 4"), curved at 15°, with a 5° pitch. The design failed to capture enough wind energy to spin, producing 0 watts. Learned that we needed to scale up the blade length and increase the twist to capture more airflow.

March 7 - 9, 2023

Increasing Surface Area & Adding Blades (1.4 Watts)

Extended blades to 12 inches and increased curvature to 30°. The turbine spun well and produced 1.4 watts! However, there were large gaps between blades. We increased the blade count to 6 to capture more wind, but performance stalled at 1.4 watts due to added weight and drag.

March 10 - 13, 2023

Material Swap to Cardboard & Zero Pitch (2.7 Watts)

Removed blade pitch and extended length to 14"–15". Power jumped to 2.5 watts. On March 13, we swapped the chipboard for cardboard, which made the blades more stable and effective. Output increased to 2.7 watts, though drag remained high due to having 6 blades.

March 16, 2023

Final Wind Tunnel Test (7.4 Watts)

Drastically reduced drag by dropping down to 3 cardboard blades (14" long, 10° curve, 0° pitch). Covered the gaps near the hub to funnel wind more effectively. Tested backwards in the wind tunnel for 20 seconds. The turbine generated a massive 7.4 Watts and a 3 Volt average. Less blades meant less wind was required to start spinning, and far less aerodynamic drag!

#12 Final Testing
3/16/23

Drawn by Duc Le · 3/16/23

Design Image

Technical Drawing

Description

3 blades attached to the hub, all 60° apart. Each blade is 3 in. wide and 14 in. long, curved at 10°, with no pitch. Material is cardboard.

Testing Procedure

Hub attached to the turbine and placed inside the wind tunnel, no pitch. Turbine faced away in the wind tunnel and run for 20 seconds; average power was determined.

Result & Improvement

Generated 7.4 watts in 20 seconds, averaging ~3 volts consistently. Fewer blades meant less wind was needed to start spinning and less drag — more power. Covering the hub–blade gap also helped. A further improvement would be optimizing the blade twist, since the current twist causes drag when facing the ground.

Final Result: We removed 3 blades, leaving 3 at 60° spacing with no pitch. Tested facing backward in the wind tunnel for 20 seconds: 7.4 watts generated, 3-volt average.

Corrections for PRO 3/17

Cork-hub windmill redesign · March 28–31, 2023

#1 Initial Design
3/28/2023

Drawn by Duc Le · 3/28/2023

Design Image

Description

Four blades measuring 5 × 3 inches with a 5-degree pitch angle, secured to tea pins 1 inch from the hub with tape. The hub is made of cork, connected to an 8-inch shaft; a string at the end holds a cup for weights.

Justification

Based on prior KidWind Challenge experience, large surface area is crucial for efficiency. A 4-blade design balances surface area and wind speed well. Cork was chosen over foam for the hub since foam wears down and causes shaft slippage over time.
