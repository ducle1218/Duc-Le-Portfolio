Volumetric Display

CAD & preliminary analysis

Designing the rotating structure for a swept-volume LED display: from a solid wall concept to a modular, 20-arm assembly.

The work so far centers on rotor geometry, printable connections, motor packaging, and early structural models. Electronics synchronization and a working display are still ahead.

01 / August 4–7

Start with the volume and its enclosure.

Early sketches explored how a rotating surface could carry LEDs and fit above a stationary base.

02 / August 7–11

Replace the wall with modular arms.

The design moved toward thinner radial supports to accommodate LEDs, make scaling easier, and reduce exposed area. Drag reduction remains a design intent.

03 / August 6–11

Design around the motor and printer.

The center assembly has to transmit motion, route power, and fit the available printer.

Design for the available machine

The center shaft was split into printable sections to fit the Prusa Mini’s approximately 7-inch build envelope. The connection is part of the design, not an afterthought.

04 / August 12

Use simulation to guide the next questions.

Two preliminary models examine different components and loads. They are starting points for refinement and physical testing.

What this result establishes

The ~23 psi value belongs to the arm model. Printed-material behavior, constraints, mesh convergence, balance, and dynamic loading still require validation; this result does not establish a safe operating speed.

Next iteration

Move from a mechanical model to tested hardware.

Print and check the joints, refine the structural assumptions, then develop guarded spin testing and LED timing. The notebook does not yet document a functioning display.
